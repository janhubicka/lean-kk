## 1. Actual class columns and the two different age notions

The finite partition is maintained by complete old Q passing columns, not
by a partial selected graph or a stipulated age label. Its classes have
constant old columns and distinct classes have different columns. A genuine
split marker separates its two nonempty children by its new bit. A cover
block is constant on each existing class. Coding takes place only after
the coded vertex is isolated and every other class has been refined by
adjacency to it; deletion of that singleton and addition of the coding
column preserve the invariant.

The canonical class age is the full age of finite gluings over the old
prefix. It need not be the age actually realized in the remaining subsets
of the fixed Henson graph V. The function F-star is initially defined from
actual clique capacities of those subsets. Actual clique witnesses show
that it is an integer-witness graph age and lies below the current gluing
age. The saturated cover chain makes the *gluing* age equal to F-star at
its endpoint. This is the reason F-star can subsequently serve as the
actual gluing pre-age; no assertion that every F-star-admissible finite
graph occurs in those residual subsets is required.

The ordinary Q existence argument remains sound under this distinction.
At each cover, the actual V neighbourhood has clique capacity bounded by
the new support value because F-star is below the intermediate target age.
The new block is nonadjacent to the entire older prefix and all other
blocks. Consequently a mixed clique meets one auxiliary block and later

singleton child or a triangle-free neighbourhood of the pending coded
vertex. Every coding round has a finite partition and a finite cover chain;
following the fixed enumeration codes every vertex after finitely many
rounds. No additional fairness or density construction is needed.
